The Create framework

FaithTech's way of working through a day like this. Four movements, in order.

  1. LamentStart with what is broken, honestly, before you try to fix it.
  2. DiscernListen and decide together. Each team names its direction.
  3. BuildMake something real. Work in short loops.
  4. StewardCare for what you made, and the people it is for.

The build loop

Pray→Prototype→Pause↻

Pray before you start a push. Prototype something small. Pause to check it, and each other. We stop together for prayer at 2:00 PM.

Pick one track

Teams of 3 to 5. Each team has a project lead and a primary developer, and everyone has a role.

Everything for the day, including the ground rules, a team README template and each track's test data, is in the hackathon repo on GitHub.

Track 1 · top priority

Mobile-first church onboarding

How might we make registering a church and reporting baptisms as easy as sending a text?

Who it's for
AI and agent builders, backend, messaging and telephony APIs, internationalization.
Done at 6 PM
A judge texts a number from their own phone. The agent answers in their language, collects church name, pastor name, email and location, confirms back, and the church appears on a live dashboard within seconds. The same number then texts a baptism count and the dashboard total moves.
Must also
Reject bad submissions (duplicates, non-numbers, impossible counts) and get the pastor's opt-in before sending anything.
Test data
Full brief and test data on GitHub (churches.csv, sample messages).
Track 2

Testimony sharing

How might we make it easy for someone who was just baptized to share their story?

Who it's for
Design, frontend, video and media, storytellers.
Done at 6 PM
Someone who was baptized opens a link, answers a few guided prompts (typed, spoken or a short video), and leaves with a testimony they can post right away, without anyone at a church touching it. If they say yes, the Baptize team can see the story and feature it.
Must also
Nothing goes public without the person's OK, and anyone under 18 needs a parent or guardian's OK.
Test data
Full brief and test data on GitHub (sample stories, testimony prompts).
Track 3 · stretch

The first 30 days

How might we walk with someone through their first 30 days after baptism?

Who it's for
Product and UX, messaging, content design, pastors and disciple-makers. The hardest of the three to get right in a day.
Done at 6 PM
A judge plays someone baptized that morning. They enroll by text or QR code in their language, get Day 1 on their phone, reply, and get the next step (fast-forward the days for the demo). Their church can see they're active, and who has gone quiet.
Must also
Every message needs opt-in and an easy way to stop. This is a build track, not a curriculum track.
Test data
Full brief and test data on GitHub (new-believers.csv, 30-day outline).

Build rules

  1. Start fresh today. Any stack.
  2. Test data only. No real church, pastor or personal data.
  3. Credit your sources.
  4. Share your work. A public GitHub repo if you can, or a link anyone can open. For stewardship, not ownership of your IP.
  5. Code freeze at 4:00 PM.

Demo at 4:30: 3 minutes, then 2 minutes of questions. Start with your track and the person you built it for, show it working on a phone, then Tend, Examine, Testify. A demo, not a pitch.

Backup video: have Claude make it (about 10 min, start by 3:30)

Open Claude Code in your project folder, fill in the four blanks, paste. Claude drives your app like a real person, records it, and cuts a 60 to 90 second video around what judges score.

Make a 60–90 second demo video of our prototype for the judges.

Team: [name] · App: [URL, or how to start it] · Track [1/2/3] · For: [one person: who, where, language, phone] · Test data only.

1. Record: drive the real app with Playwright at phone size (390×844). Do the one core task as that person, start to finish. Record the screen. No faked screens; if a step is mocked, say so on screen.
2. Edit: turn the recording into a motion-designed MP4 with Remotion (1080×1920, 30 fps, under 90 s). Beats:
   - Who (5 s): team, track, the person it's for.
   - Why (10 s): their problem in one line. → redemptive potential
   - Do it (40–60 s): the live flow, zoom to each tap, one short caption per step. → usable by the end user
   - Proof (10 s): the result landing for real (saved, dashboard moves). → technical viability
   - Real vs mocked (5 s): one honest line, plus what Baptize would need next.
   Large captions, high contrast, smooth zooms. No voiceover needed.
3. Check it: watch your own render (sample frames), fix anything unreadable, then give me the file path and length.

Then upload it (YouTube unlisted, Google Drive or Loom, "anyone with the link") and paste the link in Demo video. No code? Screen-record your phone while you use it. Full guide

Judging & honors

Judges score every demo 1 to 5 on three questions.

  • 40%Redemptive potential. Could this advance the gospel and reach real people, beyond the demo?
  • 30%Usable by the end user. Could a pastor, a new believer or a church volunteer use it without us in the room?
  • 30%Technical viability. Does it work live, end to end, and could it fit into Baptize's tech stack?

Honors are announced at the close, in this order. Each winning team gets a $100 Claude gift to keep building.

  1. Most RedemptiveThe highest score above.
  2. Most CollaborativeThe mentors' pick.
  3. Most CreativeThe judges' pick.

Baptize The World may also name an optional Baptize's Pick, chosen by Pastor Calin. Claude does a quick read of each repo or link before demos.
